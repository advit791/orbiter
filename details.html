Deep-Space Archive

For explorers, nerds, and future astronauts. Everything you need to know before leaving Earth.

The Universe
🌌

Observable Universe

Diameter: 93 billion light-years  ·  Age: 13.8 Gyr

The universe is expanding — and at the very edges, expansion is faster than light itself.

Nothing moves through space faster than light, but space itself has no speed limit.

🧊

Cosmic Microwave Background

Age of snapshot: 380,000 years post–Big Bang

The oldest light in existence. Tiny temperature ripples in the CMB seeded the galaxies we see today.

🌀

Black Holes

Escape velocity: > c (speed of light)

Gravity so intense that not even light escapes. Formed from collapsed stars or galactic cores.

Time slows near a black hole. Distant observers see you freeze at the event horizon.

⚡

Dark Energy

68% of total universe energy content

A mysterious force accelerating cosmic expansion. We can measure it, but we have no idea what it actually is.

Stars & Galaxies
🌠

Neutron Stars

Rotation: up to 700 Hz  ·  Diameter: ~20 km

Ultra-dense stellar remnants. Some spin hundreds of times per second — pulsars you can set your clock to.

A teaspoon of neutron star material weighs ~4 billion tons.

🌟

Supernovae

Peak luminosity: ~10 billion Suns

Explosive deaths of massive stars, briefly outshining entire galaxies. Every atom of gold on Earth was forged in one.

🪐

Exoplanets

Confirmed: 5,500+  ·  and counting

Worlds beyond our solar system in all shapes — some orbit two suns, some rain molten glass sideways at 5,000 mph.

🌌

Milky Way

100–400 billion stars  ·  Diameter: 100,000 ly

Our home galaxy — and it's on a slow-motion collision course with Andromeda in ~4.5 billion years.

Quantum Realm
🧪

Quantum Vacuum

Empty space is never truly empty. Quantum fields fluctuate constantly, producing virtual particles that pop in and out of existence.

⚛️

Wave-Particle Duality

Light and matter behave as both particles and waves simultaneously. Observing a quantum system changes its behaviour.

🔗

Quantum Entanglement

Two particles become linked across any distance. Measuring one instantly affects the other — what Einstein called "spooky action at a distance."

Space Travel
🚀

Rocket Propulsion

Rockets expel mass backward to push forward. Space is a vacuum — rockets work without air, but produce no sound either.

🛰️

Satellites & ISS

ISS speed: 17,500 mph — 5 miles per second

The ISS completes 16 orbits of Earth every day. Astronauts on board watch 16 sunrises and 16 sunsets daily.

🌑

Moon Missions

First landing: July 20, 1969

Apollo 11 changed history. Fun detail: Moon dust smells like burnt gunpowder when first brought into the lunar module.
